What this is: the inviscid pressure (wave) drag coefficient of the nose and the body ahead of the base, on the body's cross-sectional area, at zero incidence. It excludes skin friction, base drag and fins, so it is not a rocket's total drag. The bow shock is fitted: it is the outer boundary of the grid, with the exact jump conditions across it, and the error falls at close to second order in the cell size (observed orders 1.6 to 2.4). The value shown is the finest grid's; its band is measured from the grids and includes an allowance of 7e-5 of the value for an error the grid family cannot show. Checked against exact or converged reference solutions on families of cones (Taylor-Maccoll) and ogives (a method of characteristics) at Mach 1.3 to 5: there every band measured contains the error of the value shown, and the value extrapolated from three grids lies within 3.4e-5 of the reference (design record 0006 in the repository). Other shapes, lengths and Mach numbers are computed the same way but have not been compared with a reference. Sharp noses only for now. Refused, with the reason: tips whose shock detaches; tips whose flow crosses the solver's starting plane, just behind the tip, at an axial Mach number below 1 (of 40 noses of 1 to 3 calibers tried at Mach 1.2, 19 detach their shock and 13 are refused by this test); tips steeper than 30 deg (a limit of the present grids, under review); bodies whose flow at their end is subsonic.
