Transition: natural. From shadowgraphs at M 1.5 to 3 (air off), transition
Reynolds number about 1.6 million, independent of R for 2.9e6 < R < 5e6
(p. 13, fig. 15), "low in comparison with usual wind tunnel and free-flight
experience", possibly from surface roughness. At M 1.5 the Reynolds number at
the base is 2.2 million (p. 14).

## Stated uncertainty (pp. 6 to 7)

- Angle-of-attack correction: increments estimated within +-25%, so zero-lift
  drag in error by no more than 2.5% for most rounds (more where the
  correction is large, e.g. M 3.29 at 6.8%).
- Random: within-round scatter averages about +-3%; scatter about the faired
  curve "less than +-2 percent" at M 2.
- Systematic: less than 1%.

Reference area: body base area (equal to the cross-section, cylindrical body).

## Digitised CD at zero lift (fig. 11, PDF p. 33)

Procedure: 300 dpi render; CD gridlines every 0.1 at 223.4 px (CD = 0 at y =
1671); Mach gridlines every 1 at 224.5 px (M = 0 at x = 570, fitted to the
gridlines from M 1 to 10). Each point is a circle with a centre dot; the dot
was located as the darkest 3 x 3 pixel spot within 3 to 4 px of a visual guess
(one dot inside overlapping rings was read from a pixel-intensity dump).
Reading uncertainty +-1 px, i.e. +-0.0005 in CD, +-0.005 in M. Below M 2 the
digitised Mach numbers sit 0.02 to 0.03 below the table I values of the same
rounds; above M 2 they agree within 0.01. Points are paired with table I
rounds by order; the two points at M 1.56 cannot be assigned individually to
the rounds at 1.57 and 1.58.

The spread from 0.446 to 0.494 at M 1.43 to 1.56 is the "bucket" the report
attributes to base drag, with transition moving across the base and the fin
shocks (pp. 11, 13 to 14).

Other results in the report used in 0007:

- Theory (head drag by characteristics, fin wave drag by R. T. Jones's method,
  flat-plate friction with the observed transition, base drag from a fairing
  of data) agrees with experiment within 5% at M 1.5 to 3, "to some degree
  fortuitous because the uncertainty in the base drag estimate is of the same
  order of magnitude" (p. 12).
- Friction by flat-plate theory at free-stream Reynolds number against a
  second method (laminar cone theory on the ogive with local Reynolds number,
  2D elsewhere): within 3% below M 7.2 (p. 10, fig. 8).
- At low Mach numbers body friction increases about fourfold from all laminar
  to all turbulent (p. 8, fig. 7).
- Blunt fin leading edge: total CD up by about 0.02 below M 5 (4 to 10% of
  total, about 100% of the fin wave drag); symmetric bevel: almost no change
  (p. 15, fig. 17).
- A nearly identical model tested at NOL (fins of constant thickness 3.13% of
  root chord) gave drag up to 20% different at M 3.5 (p. 12, fig. 13).
