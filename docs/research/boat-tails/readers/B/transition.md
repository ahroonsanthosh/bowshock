### Q8. Completely boattailed bodies (PDF page 11, report p. 10)

> "The boundary-layer profiles on the completely boattailed bodies (fig. 18(b)) were again apparently turbulent but were all distorted from the one-seventh power profile possibly as a result of the longer run in the presence of adverse pressure gradient or as a result of the increased ratio of boundary-layer thickness to base radius, or both. ### Q12. Base pressure coefficient point called a deviation; size of the wire effect (PDF page 15, report p. 14)

The paragraph opens: "The experimental base pressure coefficients Cp,b and C'p,b determined from the pressure data of this report are presented in figure 24 as functions of boattail angle for all configurations tested." It continues:

> "The agreement with the data presented herein is fairly good. Deviation of the experimental value of C'p,b for the epsilon = 5.63 deg boattail with Db/Dm = 0.704 may be qualitatively explained by the fact that early boundary-layer transition resulted in a large increase in boundary-layer thickness ahead of the base. (The effect of forced boundary-layer transition at the model tips was to increase the base pressure coefficients approximately 0.015.)"

- First sentence: applies to the figure 24 point C'p,b at epsilon = 5.63 deg, Db/Dm = 0.704 (stated). The report calls it a "Deviation". It says the deviation "may be qualitatively explained" by early boundary-layer transition.
- It does not say whether the Cp,b point at the same epsilon and Db/Dm (figure 24) is also affected.
- Parenthesis ("approximately 0.015"): which configurations, which coefficient (Cp,b or C'p,b), and whether any wire points are plotted in figure 24: not stated.

The same paragraph also says:

> "A single value of C'p,b obtained from the tests of the parabolic body of revolution of reference 6 is included for comparison at M = 1.9."

This gives the Mach number (1.9) and coefficient (C'p,b) of the figure 24 "Model from reference 6" symbol. In my `rme51f26_fig24.csv` I left that point's Mach number empty and its coefficient type open, because the figure itself gives neither (see section 3).

### Q13. Base pressure drag point called inconsistent (PDF page 19, report p. 18)

> "Base pressure drag was observed to increase with decreasing boattail angle for the subject base to body diameter ratios. This variation is in accordance with the method of this report, although the method overestimated the base drag as a result of the deviation of theoretical and experimental pressures ahead of the base. The data point for epsilon = 5.63 deg, Db/Dm = 0.704 is the point previously indicated as inconsistent as a result of inadvertent early transition of the boundary layer on the body."

- Applies to: the base pressure drag data point at epsilon = 5.63 deg, Db/Dm = 0.704 (stated). The report calls it "inconsistent" and gives the cause as "inadvertent early transition of the boundary layer on the body".
- Figure number: not stated in this paragraph on PDF page 19. The figures are introduced on PDF page 18, which I did not open.
- Whether the side pressure drag or total pressure drag points at the same epsilon and Db/Dm are affected: not stated.

### Q14. Estimated value at zero boattail angle (PDF page 19, report p. 18)

> "The experimental value of base pressure drag coefficient at a boattail angle of zero was estimated by assuming that the base pressure obtained with the cylindrical afterbody would be the limiting pressure. Actually this indication would not be true since epsilon = 0 corresponds to an indefinitely long boattail with an indefinitely thick boundary layer, which would indicate that the base pressure might approach ambient pressure (that is, CD = 0). The actual variation might be expected to deviate sharply from the high pressure drag coefficient toward zero at small boattail angles."

- Applies to: the experimental base pressure drag coefficient at a boattail angle of zero (stated). The report says it was "estimated" from the cylindrical-afterbody base pressure and that "this indication would not be true".
- Figure number: not stated in this paragraph on PDF page 19.
