## 3. How I read the figures (all reports)

**Images.** Pages were rendered with `pdftoppm -r 300` (greyscale PNG). For TM X-1960 figs. 7 and 11 I used the native embedded scans (`pdfimages`, bilevel, 300 ppi), rotated them to landscape and removed the small scan rotation (deskew by maximising the sharpness of the row and column ink profiles of the grid: p. 38 -0.575 deg, p. 39 -2.550 deg, p. 40 -0.500 deg, p. 41 -1.450 deg, p. 48 +0.300 deg), because a rotation of a degree or two would otherwise tilt the axes by several pixels across the plot.

**Axis calibration.** Two methods, both listed figure by figure in Appendix B with pixel positions and residuals.
- Grid-line fit (`scripts/digit.py`, positions from `scripts/panelcal.py`): every labelled grid line (or tick) is sampled along its length, each sample's position is the darkness-weighted centre across the line, and value = a + b x px + c x py is fitted by least squares over all samples of all labelled lines of that axis. The affine form absorbs a small scan rotation. The residual of each line and the rms in axis units are kept.
- Comb fit (`scripts/periodic.py`), used on the TM X-1960 bilevel scans where the fine grid is dashed: the bold grid lines are fitted as an evenly spaced comb (spacing and phase searched, then each tooth refined to its darkness-weighted centre and a straight line fitted through the tooth positions). The labelled lines identify the teeth (for example tooth 0 = M 0.2 and tooth 22 = M 1.3). Calibration is the fitted comb; the tooth residuals (rms 0.20 to 0.45 px, largest 0.84 px) are in Appendix B.

**Symbol centres.** In order of preference:
1. Template fit (`scripts/digit.py`): an outline of the legend's shape (circle, square, diamond, triangle, right triangle) is matched to a lightly blurred ink map over sub-pixel positions and a small range of sizes; the score is the ink on the outline minus the ink on outlines 2.5 px inside and outside. Used where the symbol stands clear.
2. Fixed-size sector Hough (`scripts/circles.py`, `scripts/houghst.py`): the symbol size is fixed from clean symbols of the same figure and the centre is the position with the most 10-degree sectors of stroke ink on the outline. Used to confirm template fits and where grid lines pull the template.
3. By eye on enlarged crops with a pixel reference grid (`scripts/zoomgrid.py`, `scripts/zgsheet.py`, `scripts/tiles.py`), on ink maps printed character by character (`scripts/ascii.py`), and on crops with the regular grid faded (`scripts/combmask.py`, `scripts/degrid.py`). Used for symbols merged with grid lines, frame lines, curves or other symbols: I located the symbol's own edges or vertices and took the centre from them with the symbol size measured on clean symbols of the same figure.
4. Stack extents (`scripts/stackext.py`, `scripts/blobfind.py`): where several symbols at one station merge into one ink blob and no single symbol can be separated from its own strokes, the value is the centre of the blob and the alternatives are the highest and lowest centres a symbol inside the blob could have (blob edge plus or minus the symbol's outer radius). These points are flagged `ambiguous`.

Every fit was checked on an overlay of the fitted outline on the page; where the overlay did not sit on the symbol's strokes I replaced the fit by a reading from the strokes (methods 2 to 4) and said so in `note`.

**Centre conventions.** Circle, square, diamond: geometric centre. Equilateral triangles and right triangles: the centroid (one third of the height above the base, and for a right triangle one third of the width from the right-angle corner), as in all my readings; where a pin dot is visible (RM L54C16) it sits at the centroid, which supports this. The bounding-box centre would read about one sixth of the symbol height higher; the size of that difference is given in the note of each such point. Other shapes (the quarter-round "D" symbol of TM X-1960 fig. 7(c)): bounding-box centre.

**Reading error.** Each row's note gives a one-standard-deviation reading error: the centre uncertainty in pixels (1 px for clean template fits, 1.5 to 3 px for strokes read by eye or partly hidden symbols, more for stacks, chosen per point) times the axis scale, combined with the calibration rms. Alternatives are converted with the same calibration as the main reading.

**Abscissa given, not read.** Where the abscissa is the free-stream Mach number and the symbols sit at the tested Mach numbers (TN D-6789 figs. 12 and 20, TM X-1960 figs. 7 and 11), `x` is the Mach number as given and `x_quantity` says so. For TM X-1960 the note also gives where the symbol centre was actually drawn on the scale (within 0.006 of the nominal Mach number in every case).

**Flags.** `ok`: one clear reading. `ambiguous`: overlapping or hidden symbols, stacks, uncertain jet state or uncertain assignment; every plausible other reading is in `alternatives` and Appendix A says what I saw. `illegible`: value left empty. I did not use `curve` or `untraced`: no curve was asked for by name, and no fairing or theory line was read.

**Signs.** Every pressure coefficient was read with the sign of the axis label at its own grid line (addendum item 1); I assumed no sign. Positive pressure coefficients occur in RM L54C16 fig. 5 (3 points), RM E57E06 figs. 7 and 14 (3 and 8 points) and TN D-6789 figs. 7 and 18 (1 point each), and are given as read.
