================================================================

0014 10.7 (ag): NACA RM L54C16, RM L54I22, RM E57E06, NASA TN D-6789 and
TM X-1960. Paired on 2026-10-27 by a neutral party. Boat-tails are parked
(0014, 2026-10-27 amendment to (ae) and (ag)): this pairing is a record,
and nothing is set from it.

The pairing compares reader A with reader B and with nothing else. No
computed result was opened. No value was averaged, chosen or settled:
every pair keeps both readings, both stated errors and their envelope.
No third reading was made, and no PDF page was looked at for this
pairing (the PDFs in the readers' folders were only hashed, to confirm
both readers used the same files).

2. How the points were paired
-----------------------------

Groups. Points are paired only inside one group: the figure, its part or
panel, the Mach number where a figure holds several, and the series the
legend names. The groups are:

  RM L54C16 fig. 5      part; model (1, 2, 3); open or filled symbol
  RM L54I22 fig. 2      part (Mach number); shroud
  RM E57E06 fig. 6      part; double strut, single strut, ref. 10, other
  RM E57E06 figs. 7, 8  part; Mach; meridian (behind strut = 0, 90 deg)
  RM E57E06 fig. 14     part; Mach; open or solid; db/dj; 45 deg bevel
  TN D-6789 figs. 5, 18 Mach; meridian
  TN D-6789 fig. 7      part; l/dm panel; Mach (jet-off circle)
  TN D-6789 fig. 12     l/dm panel; beta
  TN D-6789 fig. 20     one group; the repeat-data flag compared as a label
  TM X-1960 fig. 5      meridian (M 1.30)
  TM X-1960 fig. 6      beta (M 1.30)
  TM X-1960 fig. 7      part; beta as the legend names it
  TM X-1960 fig. 11     balance square or computed circle; flag as a label
  TM X-1960 fig. 18     meridian, or the solid base-pressure triangle

Stations. Inside a group, the abscissae of both readers are joined into
station clusters wherever neighbours lie within the figure's tolerance:
0.01 in x/L (RM L54C16 fig. 5(a), RM L54I22 fig. 2), 1 deg (RM L54C16
fig. 5(b)), 0.01 in 1 - (r/rB)^2 (RM E57E06 figs. 6(a) and 14), 0.5 in
Pj/P0 (fig. 6(b)), 0.1 in (fig. 7), 0.008 in y/dB (fig. 8), 0.012, 0.015
and 0.02 in x/dm (TN D-6789 figs. 5, 7, 18), 0.015 in x/dm (TM X-1960
figs. 5, 6), 0.03 in x/dm (TM X-1960 fig. 18) and 0.02 in Mach number
(TN D-6789 figs. 12, 20; TM X-1960 figs. 7, 11). Each tolerance is below
the spacing of the figure's stations. Inside a cluster the pairing keeps
as many pairs as the smaller count allows; among those, the one with the
least summed squared distance measured in the two readers' summed errors
(with one unit added for each pair whose repeat-data flags differ, so
that a pairing keeping the flags together wins when the positions are
about as near). What is left over is read once.

Agreement. On each coordinate, the difference is within the two stated
one-sigma errors summed: A's "reading error 1-sigma x .., y .." and B's
"reading error 1 sd: x +-.., y +-.." in each row's note. Where a reader
gives the abscissa as a category and not a reading (B: the Mach number
as given in TN D-6789 figs. 12 and 20 and TM X-1960 figs. 7 and 11; A:
the nominal station or Mach number of a point it could not read), x is
not tested and the pair says so.

Envelope. Each pair gives the lowest and highest of the two readings on
each coordinate. A second y envelope also takes in every alternative
that either reader wrote as a number in a fixed form (B: "y=N (...)" and
"x=N y=N (...)"; A: a bare number, "x N, y N (...)" and "y N if ...").
All alternatives are also copied verbatim. The alternatives_check column
says where one reading agrees, within the summed errors, with one of the
other reader's numeric alternatives, or lies inside the range of the
other's reading and alternatives. This is a record, not a choice.
