4. STATED UNCERTAINTY AND PRECISION (word for word, PDF page)
-------------------------------------------------------------
p9, PRECISION: "The uncertainty of the experimental data from tunnels No. 1 and
No. 2 was calculated by considering the possible errors in the individual
measurements which entered into the determination of the stream
characteristics, pressure distributions, and drag. The final uncertainty in a
quantity was taken as the square root of the sum of the squares of the possible
errors in the individual measurements. The resulting uncertainties in the final
quantities are as follows:
  Quantity   Uncertainty
  P          +-0.004
  C_DF       +-0.004
  alpha      +-0.15 deg"
p9: "The variation of the free-stream Mach number over the length of each model
tested was less than +-0.01 for all test Mach numbers. The uncertainty in the
Mach number at a given point in the stream is +-0.003."
p9: "The magnitude of the calculated uncertainty in the drag coefficient appears
rather large relative to the observed scatter of the data. Drag coefficients
for repeated tests generally agreed within +-0.002. It is therefore believed
that the drags of models relative to one another are sufficiently accurate for
comparative purposes, although the absolute magnitudes of the drag
coefficients for the models at a particular Mach number may be in error by the
magnitude of the uncertainty."
p9: "Since there are no known systematic errors in the data from the
free-flight tunnel, the accuracy of the results is indicated by the
repeatability of the data. Examination of these data shows that repeat firings
of similar models under almost identical conditions of Reynolds number and
Mach number yielded results for which the average deviation from the faired
curve was 1 percent and the maximum deviation was 4 percent."
p9, REDUCTION OF DATA: "All the experimental data have been reduced to
coefficient form and the data from wind tunnels Nos. 1 and 2 have been
corrected for the effects of the small nonuniformities in the wind-tunnel flow.
The free-stream static-pressure variations in the model-free tunnel have been
applied as corrections to the drag and pressure-distribution data by simple
linear superposition. Corrections due to the effects of stream-angle variation
were well within the limits of accuracy of the data and have therefore been
neglected. No corrections were necessary for the data obtained in the
free-flight tunnel"
p8 (free flight): "This report includes only the data from models which had
maximum observed angles of attack of less than 3 deg, since larger angles
measurably increased the drag."
p8: Reynolds number limits of variation: see the table in section 3 (+- values).
Model dimensions: no tolerance or precision is stated anywhere I could find
("not stated").
Total drag coefficient C_D (free flight): only the repeatability statement above.
Wave drag from pressure distributions: no separate uncertainty stated ("not stated").


5. HOW EACH TRANSCRIBED QUANTITY WAS OBTAINED (word for word, PDF page)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Pressure coefficient P (fig 4):
  p8: "Pressure distributions for the hemisphere-cone pressure models (models
  4p, 5p, and 6p) were determined through the use of a liquid manometer system
  connected to two rows of orifices along the models and spaced 180 deg apart.
  The models were rotated and a" / p8 right: "longitudinal pressure distribution
  at each 30 deg increment in circumferential angle was obtained. The resulting
  pressure coefficients at each longitudinal station were averaged to obtain
  the values presented."
  p6 symbols: "P pressure coefficient, (p_l - p)/q".
  p10: "The data are referred to the free-stream Mach number ahead of the normal
  shock wave at the nose of each model. Although these Mach numbers were
  approximately the same for each model, they differed slightly because of the
  differences in positions of the models within the test section."

Foredrag C_DF, wind tunnels (fig 7(a), fig 8 circles, fig 10, fig 16):
  measured with a balance; base drag removed by measured base pressure.
  p7 right / p8 left: "Wind tunnels No. 1 and No. 2.-The total drag was measured
  by means of a strain-gage balance located in the model support housing. The
  base pressure was determined through the use of a liquid manometer connected
  to two holes in the supporting sting at the base of the model. Experimental
  values of foredrag were then taken as the difference between the measured
  total drag and base pressure drag."
  p6 symbols: "C_DF foredrag coefficient based on base area, (total drag-base drag)/qA"
  Friction: not stated explicitly for the measured foredrag (the foredrag is
  balance total drag minus base pressure drag; the report does not say friction
  is removed). Base drag: removed, by the measured base pressure as quoted.
  p19 (fig 16): "In figure 16 a comparison of the variation of foredrag
  coefficient with Mach number for all the force models tested is shown."
  p13: "The data from figure 7(a) are replotted in figure 8 to show the
  variation of foredrag with nose bluntness at constant Mach numbers and
  provide comparisons with the estimated foredrag characteristics."
  p15: "In order to verify these results, tests have been made at Mach numbers
  1.44 and 1.99 for a series of hemispherically blunted cones, formed by
  progressively blunting an L/D=3 cone. Both the experimental foredrag results
  and the estimated values of foredrag are plotted in figure 10."

Total drag C_D, free flight (fig 7(b)):
  p8: "Drag coefficient was obtained by recording the time-distance history of
  the flight of the model with the aid of a chronograph and four shadowgraph
  stations at 5-foot intervals along the test section. From these data,
  deceleration was computed and converted to drag coefficient."
  p6 symbols: "C_D total drag coefficient, total drag/qA".
  p13: "Variation with Mach number of the total drag coefficient (fig. 7(b))^4
  is similar for all models in that the drag coefficient continually decreased
  with increasing Mach number." Footnote 4: "No attempt has been made to join
  the air-off data and air-on data because of the differences in Reynolds
  number, recovery temperature, and stream turbulence,"
  Friction and base: not stated as removed (quantity is the total drag); "not stated"
  beyond the symbol definition.

Wave drag (press. distr. models) (fig 8 squares, part A) and fig 5 symbols:
  p11: "Values computed from this equation are compared in figure 5 with
  estimates of the wave drag from total drag measurements (refs. 16 and 17) and
  with the experimental pressure drag determined from the pressure
  distributions of figure 4."
  p15: "The estimated wave drag coefficients for the d/D=0.30 and 0.50 models at
  Mach numbers of 3.06 and 1.96 are in very good agreement with the wave drag
  determined from the pressure distribution models (see fig. 8)."
  Method of getting drag from the pressures (integration rule, stations used,
  treatment of the base): not stated. Friction: not stated (a pressure drag by
  description). Base: not stated.
  p6 symbols: "C_DW wave drag coefficient, wave drag/qA".

Fig 5 curves (estimates, read because Part B asks for every plotted series):
  solid "Estimated from experiment, ref. 16 and 17": p11 "estimates of the wave
  drag from total drag measurements (refs. 16 and 17)"; how friction and base
  were removed in those references: not stated in this report.
  dashed "Empirical formula, C_DW=(2P_t-1)/3": p11 "A simple expression for the
  wave drag coefficient of the hemisphere results from this empirical equation
  for the pressure distribution. Thus, based upon the maximum cross-section
  area, C_DW = (2P_t - 1)/3 (1)"; P_t "pitot-pressure coefficient" (p6).

8. DISAGREEMENTS INSIDE THE REPORT (recorded, not resolved)
-----------------------------------------------------------
a) Fig 4(c) prints M=3.80 (p10 text also says "Mach numbers between 1.5 and 3.8"),
   but p8 says the pressure tests were at "Mach numbers of 1.5, 2, 3, and 3.7".
   Flag DISAGREE on the 14 M=3.80 rows of fig 4(c).
b) Fig 8 plots a model 4p (d/D 0.30) wave drag square in the M=3.06 panel, while
   fig 4(a) gives M=3.10 for model 4p. Flag DISAGREE on that row. The M=1.96 panel
   squares come from tests figure 4 labels M=1.97 (0.01 difference, noted only).
c) Fig 7(b): three air-off circles read at Mach about 1.06, 1.13 and 1.15 (x axis),
   below the "1.2 to 4.2" range in p8. Flag DISAGREE. (Two more circles read at
   1.195 and 1.198 are within my reading error of 1.2 and are not flagged.)
d) Fig 10 caption: "constant cone angle=18 deg 56'"; figure 1(b) sketch prints
   omega=9 deg 28' and p6 defines omega as "cone half angle, deg". 18 deg 56' is
   twice 9 deg 28', so the caption likely gives the full angle; recorded in the
   geometry notes, not resolved.
e) Fig 8, M=1.44 panel: prints two Reynolds numbers (1.17x10^6 & 3.14x10^6) with no
   key to which symbol is which; reynolds left blank and flagged SERIES on those 6
   rows. Observation only: fig 7(a) shows the M=1.44 column tied to Re=3.14x10^6
   alone.
f) Fig 16 key prints d/D for models 3, 4, 8, 9 as "150" and "300" (no decimal point).
g) Fig 16 y-axis label reads "Foredrag coefficient, C_DF" with a small mark next to
   the C that may be a prime (p6 defines C'_DF as the volume-based coefficient). I
   could not decide; recorded in quantity_label.
