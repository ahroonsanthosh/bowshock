### Subsonic and transonic totals: not before launch, and say why

as a sting is zero (the half-body theorem 0019 section 3.2 (L1) itself uses),

fins' friction from correlations, a separate record with its own

users whose rockets never pass Mach 1 leave; they would leave anyway after

## 2. Mach 1.0

### What the record says

- The far boundaries dominate at Mach 1: on the regridded base grid the
  drag is 0.0898, 0.0917, 0.0928, 0.0931 at 55.1, 100.6, 224.2 and 408.7
  radii; a power law gives q 1.23 to 1.35 and C_inf 0.09343 to 0.09335; 800
  radii diverges in the near-sonic axis cells (0013 "Mach 1.0: the far
  boundaries' distance dominates"; "What did not work").
- The rule that extrapolated a correction failed its test at Mach 1.2
  because it read regridding noise as signal (0013 certification plan,
  "Result of the test"). The nested-domain allowance rule replaced it, as a
  bound: negligible if the larger change is within a quarter of the band;
  geometric remainder |c2| / (1 - rho) if rho = |c2| / |c1| is at most 0.6;
  else refused (`src/verification/domain-allowance.ts`, DECAY_LIMIT 0.6).
  Its pre-registered text names the next step: "Moving the family to 224.2
  radii, with 408.7 as the third distance, is the next step if refusals here
  matter. It costs about 1.2 times the cells" (0013 "The allowance rule").
- Its test (a) passed: nested domains move the cone's drag at Mach 1.2 and
  1.1 by under 1.1e-7. Test (b): every bound given contains the larger
  domain's change; Mach 1.01 passes with rho 0.32 and an allowance of 1.3%;
  Mach 1.02 is refused on a sign change; Mach 1.0 was running (0013 "Build
  progress", step 1). You report that at Mach 1.0 on nested domains rho is
  0.63, so the rule refuses.
- The sonic far field: 0013 "Mach 1 without the extrapolation" derives n =
  3m - 2 for a potential r^n F(x / r^m), with m 2/3 for a drag-carrying far
  field and 1/2 for the sting's displaced mass, and measures local decay
  exponents 0.39 to 0.47 between 19 and 62 radii with no collapse for any m
  from 0.4 to 1 within 40 radii. The Barish and Guderley solution is "not
  read".
- The fourth grid: r = 4 had not settled after 89,200 steps and its value
  is a fitted estimate; r = 8 is about 300,000 steps, 40 hours on one core,
  8 to 25 minutes on a mid-range GPU by estimate (0013 "Mach 1.0 itself");
  the two-float GPU path costs 3.7 times the 32-bit step (0013 step 4b).

### Recommendation

**Move the family's own domain to 224.2 radii at Mach 1.0 (and 1.01), with
100.6 as the smaller and 408.7 as the larger nested domain, and apply the
rule as written.** On the regridded numbers c1 = +1.076e-3 and c2 =
+3.07e-4, rho = 0.29, allowance 4.3e-4, 0.5% of the drag. Even if the nested
ratio is twice that it passes. This is the rule's own pre-registered
fallback, so it is not a change made after seeing the result; DECAY_LIMIT
stays 0.6.

Evidence to record with it, in this order:

1. The nested base-grid solve at 408.7 radii at Mach 1.0 (46,700 plain
   steps, 654 s wall time on the regridded grid, 0013's table) and the three
   nested changes 55.1 to 100.6, 100.6 to 224.2, 224.2 to 408.7: show the
   ratios fall (rho_1 then rho_2 smaller), as a power law with q above 1
   and growing distance ratios implies.
2. The rule's test (b) cannot be run for the moved family in the form
   written (it would need 800 radii). Record instead that the geometric
   bound from (55.1, 100.6, 224.2) at Mach 1.0, computed with the measured
   rho of 0.63 although the rule refuses it, contains C(408.7) - C(100.6):
   the form of the decay holds there, which is what the rule rests on. Say
   plainly that this is a consistency check, not the registered test.
3. The physical reason the decay continues: a free-stream condition at
   distance D is wrong by the perturbation there, which falls at least as
   the measured exponents and asymptotically faster (0013's derivation);
   the drag's error fell as D^-1.06 (ogive) to D^-1.35 (cone) over four
   distances. Cite it as the ordering for the remainder.
4. The grid band: Mach 1.0 needs r = 8 for a four-grid band (0013 "Mach 1.0
   itself"). Serve it the way rule 8 of 0013 "Serving below Mach 1.2"
   decides: the page states the cost and the user chooses; a point with
   three grids is "no band" and refused, as now.
5. Two cheap consistency checks, not bands: Bryson's stationarity gives a
   forebody's drag a positive slope through Mach 1 (TR 1094 eq. 43, which
   0019 section 3.2 already uses), so C_D(1.00) must lie below C_D(1.01),
   which it does (0.0932 against 0.1049); and slender-body theory's slope
   1.64 against the three-point estimate 1.49 to 1.54 (0013 "Near Mach 1").
   Both go in the label as checks.

**Do not build the Guderley far field for launch.** Three reasons: the
asymptotic regime is not reached where the condition would sit (above); it
is a small-disturbance asymptotic form imposed on an Euler solve, so its
own modelling error would need measuring against something, and the only
something is the large-domain solve you already have; and the record says
the source is "not read" and the form "to be verified against the sources
first". It is a good later project.

**Do not reinstate extrapolation as a correction**, even on nested domains.
It was refuted once (on regridded domains, for a reason the nested grids
remove), and a bound serves the owner's rule better than a correction: the
band is the whole remainder, not an estimate of it. Keep the power-law fit
as a diagnostic in the record.

Risk if wrong: on nested grids the decay beyond 224.2 radii may be slower
than the regridded 0.29 suggests, since the regridded changes carried
noise of either sign; if rho exceeds 0.6 there too, Mach 1.0 stays refused
and the 800-radius divergence (the near-sonic axis cells) becomes the work.
The characteristic far field is degenerate at exactly sonic inflow (u_n = a
is a zero characteristic speed; the switch 0013 tried to blend), which is
likely why 800 radii diverges and why Mach 1.01 and 1.02 behave better than
1.00; a Mach 1.0 point is a measure-zero instant of a flight, and the
rocketeer needs 1.0 to 1.1 densely more than 1.0 exactly. If the moved
family fails, serve 1.01 and 1.02 and refuse 1.00 with that reason.

## 3. Surface-subsonic tangent tips

### What the record says

- The fitted solver imposes the tip cone's conical state on an inflow plane
  at x0 = 1e-3 of the nose length. It refuses tips whose flow crosses that
  plane axially subsonic, except tangent noses whose surface flow is
  supersonic (0006 "The flow behind the tip must cross the starting plane
  supersonically"; "Served: a tangent nose whose tip flow is supersonic but
  axially subsonic"). The 3-caliber tangent ogive at Mach 1.2 (18.9 deg
  tip) has conical surface flow at Mach 0.85 (0006 line 447): subsonic on
  the surface, not near sonic. The parabola of parameter 1 at Mach 1.25 has
  a tip of 18.4 deg and a similar state.
- What was measured with the refusal removed (0006 "What serving these tips
  would need"): the inflow plane is not the obstacle; moved from 1e-4 to
  1e-2 of the nose length with the columns held, the drag at x0 = 1e-3
  differs from 1e-4 by at most 1.3e-6 on that ogive, falling as x0 squared
  or faster, and the conical state imposed there, about 1% off the true
  flow, is forgotten within ten x0. The shoulder (a corner) is the
  obstacle, so cone-cylinders stay refused. Tangent ogives converge at
  observed orders 2.69 (3 calibers, Mach 1.2) and 2.15 (Mach 1.35); the
  captured-shock bands (2.9e-3, 2.3e-3) contain them but are no reference
  at the fitted precision.
- The served exception at Mach 1.35 rests on four grids, two families
  within 1.5e-5, the captured-shock band containing both, and the inflow
  plane moved (2.4e-5 or less on nj = 20). The second reference on
  `subtip-tangent` (2 calibers at Mach 1.6, surface Mach 1.0036, axial
  0.886) has two families within 2.0e-6 and no captured-shock band at all
  (0006 on that branch, "A second reference of that class"). Cases A and P
  are "being measured" there; you report two families within 7e-5, which
  is the floor (`FITTED_DRAG_FLOOR`, 0006 line 346).
- The procedure caps the observed order at 2 for the band (0006 line 946).

### Recommendation

The transonic solve is the right kind of check (no start plane, a captured
shock, a characteristic far field, different grids) and it is independent in
the one respect that matters here, the start plane. It is sufficient to rule
out a gross error; it is not sufficient to admit the fitted band at 1e-4,
because its own certified bands at Mach 1.2 are 3.7e-5 on the cone and
1.1e-3 on the ogive (0013 "The band at page sizes"). Require, for each
admitted class:

1. **The transonic solve at its own certified standard**: four grids, the
   procedure's band, the far-field and cylinder allowances, and the fitted
   extrapolation inside that band. Record the difference and label the
   served value "cross-checked by a captured-shock solve within D".
2. **The two start-plane sensitivities of 0006, on the served family, in
   the record**: x0 moved with the columns held, and a deliberately
   perturbed imposed state (the conical state of a tip 1 deg steeper, or
   the state from the previous grid's solution) with the drag unchanged
   within the band. The second is the direct test of "forgotten within ten
   x0" for a subsonic tip region, which is elliptic: the measured decay of
   the start state's influence over a distance of order x0 is the
   argument, and it should be measured on the case served, not quoted from
   the Mach 1.35 ogive. (Aerodynamic reasoning, not the record: on a
   subsonic tip the influence of a boundary of size x0 decays like a
   potential over distances of order x0, which is why the drag's
   sensitivity fell as x0 squared. The argument is sound; make the
   measurement show it on A and P.)
3. **An admission domain bounded by what is measured**: tangent noses only
   (as now), tip cone surface Mach not below the least measured (0.85 on
   case A), attached tip shock with the same margin the refusal tests, and
   no extrapolation to steeper tips or lower Mach numbers without a new
   reference of this kind. State the measured cases on the page as 0006
   and the README already do for Mach 1.35 and 1.6.
4. **The families' agreement against the floor.** The floor of 7e-5 is
   "about twice the largest error measured of the procedure's own
   extrapolation" on supersonic tips (README.md lines 273 to 279). If the two
   families on A or P differ by more than half of it, widen the served band
   to their difference rather than admit at the floor; if the two triples'
   orders differ by more than the procedure's 0.5 (the 2.69 at Mach 1.2 is
   above the scheme's order, a sign the family is not yet asymptotic),
   refuse, as the procedure does.
5. **One measured sanity check** against gross error: the 3-caliber ogive
   pressures of NASA TN D-3516 that 0013 lists as not digitised, or TN
   3716's ogive at the lowest Mach it covers; useless at 1e-4 but it would
   catch a tip-region error of a few percent, which neither family can.

Risk if wrong: a common-mode error in the start-plane model shared by both
families and below the transonic check's 1e-3. The label carries that
precision, so the claim is bounded by what was checked; and the population
cost of keeping these tips refused is small (axially subsonic tips are 2.1%
of cases at Mach 1.2 to 1.5 and the sole blocker of none, `impact.out` line
50), so there is no coverage reason to hurry.

### Verdicts

- **Refuse near free-stream Mach 1 and near trailing-edge normal Mach 1:
  right.** The xi_TE rule is the honest statement that the linearised sink
  has no meaning in the transonic range, and the Mach 1.06 floor keeps the
  body's base from reaching the fins' noses upstream; both are argued, not
  fitted.
- **Factor 2 on the lower bound: defensible, keep it.** The margin and the
  xi_TE threshold of 1.175 are one argument (the local beta may fall by at
  most root 2 where the nonlinear term takes at most half of 1 - M_TE^2),
  the infinite line sink overstates the effect for a finite fin, and full
  closure of the wake is the largest sink the plate can present. The first
  look at R&M 3348 fig. 31b (-0.10 to -0.15 measured against -0.12 to -0.17
  modelled) sits inside the factor. Round 10 has now read fig. 31b a second
  time: pair the readings and record the check before wiring, as 3.2
  already promises.
- **Reynolds allowance, Barrowman note, measured values in the R&M 3348
  gap: right.** On Barrowman, say it in the page's words: "OpenRocket uses
  Barrowman's curve, a cylinder's total drag less a base-drag curve, which
  gives 0.53 here; bowshock's band is -0.07 to 0.83 and contains it; the
  nominal differs (0.38) because the band is the whole gap between a lower
  and an upper bound, not a fit" (0019 section 3.2 (U5)).
- **Serving rounded edges below M_n 1.0 as "bounded, not measured": I
  would not, yet.** Three reasons. (i) The ordering (U3) is the weakest in
  the project: the bound C(1.0) is a plate's value only from M_n 1.0 up
  (CR-149372's cylinder flow, 0016 section 11), and below it the plate's
  nose is argued from estimates built on a cylinder's pressures, one of
  which exceeds the bound at 0.8; 10.2 asks for the ordering "shown by a
  measurement or by a physical argument", and (U3) is an analogy with a
  stated exception. (ii) The gain is 6.9% of the affected edges, and in
  total terms nothing, since those cases are refused several times over at
  Mach 1.0 to 1.2 (section 4 of 0019: "Totals at Mach 1.0 to 1.2 are 0%
  today and stay 0% until those land"). (iii) A measurement may exist:
  NASA TM X-653 (fins and flares with fin leading-edge bluntness, M 0.6 to
  3.8) is listed as not opened (0019 section 2.3); the AEDC fin tests are
  blocked by DTIC. Name TM X-653 in 10.7, open it, and if it measures a
  rounded plate edge at subsonic normal Mach numbers the term becomes
  "estimated on component data" instead of bounded; if it does not, the
  bounded term goes in with (U3)'s weakness named in its label. The
  coordinator's decision to wire nothing until the reviewer passes the
  rewrite is right; add TM X-653 to what the reviewer sees.
- **The cap at M_n 0.8 pending CR-149372 fig. 8a**: round 10 has read fig.
  8a (`round10/method.md`, "NASA CR-149372 (Macha), fig. 8a"), so the
  pairing can be made now. Decide the cap on it, as 3.2 says, before the
  PARD range A run, because that run loses Mach 1.13 to 1.41 to the cap
  (0.8 at 45 deg sweep is Mach 1.13; normal Mach 1.0 is Mach 1.41). That
  gap, not coverage, is the real cost of the cap.
- **Priority within W7**: F4 and F1 first, F3 (bevels, now measurable on
  fig. 32) second, F5 third, F2 last. 0019 section 6 has F4 first already;
  move F1 up, since nothing below 1.5 is served without it.

Risk if wrong on F2: if the bounded term is served and (U3) fails at M_n
0.75 to 0.8 (the plate's nose fuller than the cylinder's by a fifth), the
served nominal is still inside the band's width in most cases (the band is
